\section{Reproducibility note and evidence inventory}

The internally prespecified brief posed descriptive research questions
without formal statistical hypotheses or equivalence margins. Execution
expanded the learning-rate grid, added L-BFGS, and used exact outer-boundary
traces and a near-surface exclusion for both initial-rate penalties. E1b
adds retuned classical comparisons after identifying inert circuit
coordinates. Table~\ref{tab:inventory} identifies the complete evidence:
all earlier per-run records are unchanged, and all retained E3 and
secondary-wave-speed cells contain their declared seeds.

The versioned archive is
\path{QPINN_LTE_release_v1_2026-10-21.zip}. Its analysis directory contains
\path{qpinn_local.py}, \path{ground_truth.py}, \path{noise_eval.py},
\path{run_e4.py}, all launchers, \path{select_lrs.py},
\path{functional_count.py}, \path{family_equivalence.py}, the four
exact-solution verification scripts, and the table, figure, and record
generators. The results directory contains the $440$ retained per-run
JSON records collected in \path{runs_all.json} and $24$ further JSON files
of other types: $10$ segregated \texttt{*\_superseded\_*} per-run records
from discarded launches (used by no table), the current and superseded
E2/E4 aggregate files, three selected-rate summaries, and seven pilot and
diagnostic files ($464$ JSON files in all).
It also contains the trained checkpoint state dictionaries
($456$ \texttt{.pt} files, $6.9\,\mathrm{MB}$ of content: $440$ retained,
$10$ superseded, $6$ diagnostic), full-precision E2
aggregate records in \path{e2_exp_g9.json}, and corrected,
checkpoint-labeled E4 records in \path{e4_noise.json}.
The logs directory ($527$ files, $0.45\,\mathrm{MB}$) includes execution logs, queue
dispatcher outputs, and the initial-rate weight-ablation logs and variants.
The package contains the tables and figures, \path{runs_all.json} with
training histories and closure counts, verification log, coordinate-audit
output, family-equivalence check, checkpoint gains, and selected rates.
\path{RELEASE_README} maps every manuscript table and figure to its input
files and generator and gives regeneration commands and launcher thread
settings. Table~\ref{tab:inventory} separates the retained analysis records
from the provenance-only launches. The archive E2 file corresponds to the
package input \path{e2_gradvar.json}; individual E2 gradient draws were not
retained and are distinct from these aggregate records.
Repository address: \url{https://github.com/Alqahtani921893/qpinn-laser-thermoelasticity-release} (release v1, 21 October 2026); Zenodo DOI at acceptance.

\path{results_package_v6.md} is the current executed-results description;
its numerical tables are unchanged. Its protocol prose and captions use
the single-seed initial-rate ablation values, distinguish the smallest
searched spectral width from the architecture's possible minimum, give
comparator deviations separately, and avoid inferring representational
impossibility or unchanged gradient-variance decay. The corrected
\path{closed_form_appendix.md} and \path{ground_truth.py} header separate
the pulse-specific finite-difference maxima, printed inverse-transform
agreement, smooth-pulse discrepancy, and stored-reference regression
check, consistently with Table~\ref{tab:supp-verification-results}.
All are pointwise checks, not a uniform accuracy bound.

The E4 records use the corrected stress-output mapping; the earlier
incorrect evaluator outputs are excluded. The table generator groups by
pulse, wave speed, circuit, channel probability, and shot budget.
Clean controls share the noise grid and checkpoints. The verification-code
comments distinguish zero initial elastic-strain rate from nonzero
initial stress rate. The selected readout gains reported here come from
the checkpoint state-dictionary audit in the archive.

Initial E3 and secondary-speed jobs with hard-coded rates were discarded
before use, and their outputs are segregated. Completed physics sweeps
transfer the E1 exponential-pulse rates; new circuit architectures were
not independently retuned. Data-only rates were selected separately.
The circuit collocation jobs at $N_c=4096$ were stopped and dropped for
cost; this is a declared deviation from the plan (main Section~4.3). The auxiliary dispatcher skips a job when its
log exists, so completion was checked using the actual per-cell records,
not its end marker. Its adjustable worker file and overlapping queues
prevent treating all recorded durations as comparable throughput.

The reference-MLP cutoff-$0.3$ ablation used a different thread setting
from E1. Its recorded Adam history is identical, but its L-BFGS endpoint
is not. The new replay at the E1 setting reproduces all recorded numerical
histories, errors, validation values, and closure counts exactly; elapsed
time and record identifiers naturally differ (main Table~4).
The E3 anchor reruns and repeated classical architectures also reproduce
their numerical outcomes. Some anchor reruns used the auxiliary queue's
thread setting, demonstrating that the observed thread dependence is
not universal across the tested models (Table~\ref{tab:settings}; main Table~4). These checks establish reproducibility of the reported
repeats, not a guarantee of bitwise determinism for every run or platform.

The reference ablation has stress error $0.0496$ after $1060$ closures
with $2$ threads; the E1 run and its replay give $0.0461$ after $1065$
closures with $3$ threads (main Table~4). Twelve E3 anchor records
(two configurations, three families, two seeds) reproduce their E1
numerical histories and outcomes. The classical networks associated with
the two nominal-count-$84$ targets also repeat their outcomes (main
Section~4.3 and the E3 per-seed tables). These repeated executions are not
extra independent seeds.

A plausible explanation is that intra-op parallel reductions become
relevant only for sufficiently large tensor operations. The reference
network's fixed L-BFGS batch has $2048$ points and width $64$, whereas its
Adam batch has $512$ points (main Tables~2--3). A thread-dependent
floating-point summation order could then create small gradient changes
that the strong-Wolfe search amplifies into a different endpoint.
This interpretation is consistent with the phase-specific discrepancy
and smaller-model repetitions, but the operation-level reduction policy,
its grain-size threshold, and the amplification mechanism were not
separately instrumented or tested. The source declarations below are not
per-run measurements of effective thread use.

\section{Parameter accounting}

Table~\ref{tab:extended-counts} gives circuit nominal and reported
output-affecting counts and the nearest nominal-count classical designs.
Signed deviation is $100(P_{\rm classical}/P_{\rm target}-1)$.
For classical models the displayed output count equals the nominal count
under the convention that no coordinates have been structurally removed;
this is not an empirical rank claim. The released \path{functional_count.py} audits every registered scalar
at model-initialization seed $0$, with $64$ input pairs drawn uniformly
from $[-1,1]^2$ using a separate input generator seeded with $12345$.
Each scalar is changed by $+0.37$ while all others are held fixed and
then restored; a coordinate is classified as inert if the largest
absolute change of either output is below $10^{-12}$. The script's
executed output, \path{functional_parameter_audit.txt}, reports every
inert layer/qubit coordinate and the totals in
Table~\ref{tab:extended-counts}. Layer and qubit indices in that file
start at zero. The test includes encoding scales, rotation angles,
and affine readout parameters; the perturbation is an angle increment
only for the rotation coordinates. No readout coordinate is classified
as inert. Individual numerical changes are not printed by this script.
These counts describe output-affecting coordinates at the tested
initialization and inputs, not a measured Jacobian rank, intrinsic
function-space dimension, or proof that all remaining coordinates are
independent.

For $L=2$, the $4$-, $6$-, and $8$-qubit circuits realize the same output
function family under the qubit relabeling
$\{0,1,2,3\}\leftrightarrow\{0,1,2,n_q-1\}$.
The last CZ ring commutes with each measured $Z$ and cancels in its
expectation. Conjugation through the preceding CZ ring couples the
measured qubits $0$ and $1$ only to their ring neighbors $2$ and $n_q-1$;
all other local gates cancel from those expectations. This leaves the
same active-coordinate dependence for each width, with the $30$
output-affecting coordinates in Table~\ref{tab:extended-counts}.
The executed \path{family_equivalence.py} check transplants encoding
scales, rotation angles, and affine readouts of the $4$-qubit circuit
onto these qubits of the wider circuits. On $64$ random input pairs,
\path{family_equivalence_check.txt} reports maximum absolute raw-output
differences of $5.55\times10^{-16}$ and $8.88\times10^{-16}$ for $6$ and
$8$ qubits. The same transplantation at $L=4$ gives $0.811$ and $0.848$;
this control shows that the transplantation is no longer output-preserving
at that depth (Table~\ref{tab:settings}). The numerical check supports
the exact cancellation argument; it is not a measured functional rank.
Consequently, the E3
$L=2$ stress sequence $1.0127$, $1.4455$, $2.0390$ represents differently
initialized and trained realizations of one output family, with different
simulation cost, and is not a capacity trend (main Table~9).
Section~\ref{supp:reconstruction} gives the archived check and command.

Table~\ref{tab:lr-candidates} reports every final candidate score, defined
as the sum of the two relative validation errors. All selected values
minimize their recorded candidate scores and agree with production rates.
The full JSON retains intermediate Adam scores. Search-boundary selections
limit any claim of optimal tuning. E3 physics and secondary-speed runs
transfer the E1 exponential rates; data-only rates transfer from their
separately selected label count. Thus E3 is a production training-label
sweep conditional on separately selected hyperparameters. Its fixed
tuning overhead is $256$ exact training samples plus $2000$ exact
validation samples shared by the five candidate rates at $N_d=256$
(Table~\ref{tab:settings}). The tuning seed also makes these samples
common to both model families. Production runs compute validation
diagnostics, but those values neither drive parameter updates nor select
checkpoints; the final state is saved. The plotted $N_d$ is therefore
not a count of all exact-solution queries or total label-acquisition cost.
In particular, the shared routine constructs unused exact boundary data
also in the data-only regime (Table~\ref{tab:settings}); the stated tuning
overhead counts the exact samples used for training and rate selection.
